    • Questionnaire:
      • Randomization: there are two versions of the survey (A and B). Each respondent is randomly assigned to one of them with equal probability when the session starts, and the assigned version is stored in the variable version_encuesta. If a respondent resumes the survey (cookies), the version already stored is kept, so they cannot switch versions halfway through.
        • Both versions share the same beginning (consent → general information → conjoint → transition screen) and the same ending (final sociodemographic questions); they differ only in the order of the four thematic modules in between:
        Order Version A Version B
        1 Meritocracy Deservingness
        2 Attitudes towards inequality Perception of public and private services
        3 Perception of public and private services Attitudes towards inequality
        4 Deservingness Meritocracy
        • Modules and their pages in survey.qmd: Meritocracy = merit-1, merit-2; Attitudes towards inequality = clasicos, reflex, prospective, ranking; Perception of public and private services = decomodifica, lucro; Deservingness = deserve-1 to deserve-7.
        • The routing between modules is implemented with sd_skip_if() in app.R (forward jumps) and the page_next of sd_nav() in survey.qmd (backward jumps), since sd_skip_if() can only jump to pages that come later in survey.qmd.
      • Ranking of public investment areas (ranking_politicas): the order in which its 5 items are displayed is randomized for each respondent, and kept if the respondent resumes the survey (cookies). Answers are stored one variable per area (ranking_politicas_educacion, ranking_politicas_salud, etc.), regardless of the order shown.
        • ranking_order: order in which the 5 rows were displayed, one letter per area: E = Educación (education), H = Salud (health), P = Pensiones (pensions), S = Salas cunas (nurseries), C = Carreteras (roads). E.g. HCESP means Salud was shown first and Pensiones last.
    • Redirects: at the end of the survey, respondents are sent back to the fieldwork company’s panel (Nicequest) so it can assign the reward for participating. Each respondent arrives with their panel identifier in the survey link (?id_user=...), which is stored in the variable id_user and sent back as the ticket parameter of the redirect.
      • Complete: when the respondent reaches the final page (end_normal), after a 3-second countdown, they are redirected to https://transit.nicequest.com/transit/participation?tp=co_0&c=ok&ticket=<id_user>.
      • Quotafull: respondents whose quota is already full are sent, after the general information page (demographics), to the page end_quotafull, which redirects them after a 3-second countdown to https://transit.nicequest.com/transit/participation?tp=qf_1&c=ok&ticket=<id_user>.
        • Quotas are defined by category (e.g. women, men, age group, educational level), each with a target number of complete responses; a respondent is filtered out if any of the categories they belong to has already reached its target.
        • Complete responses are counted in the database as those that reached the final page (time_p_end_normal not empty).
      • Filterout: respondents who do not accept the informed consent are sent to the page end_consent, which redirects them after a 3-second countdown to https://transit.nicequest.com/transit/participation?tp=fo_0&c=ok&ticket=<id_user>.
      • Without an id_user in the link (e.g. local tests), no redirect is made and the final page only shows a closing message.

4.2 Fieldwork

4.2.1 Soft launch 1

The first soft launch was fielded by Netquest on October 30, 2026 (from 16:52, Chile time) and October 31 (a second invitation wave between 10:00 and 12:30), with a target of 500 complete responses.

Netquest In our database
Complete responses 518 469
Completes missing from our database 57 (50 with partial answers, 7 with no record at all)
Incomplete responses (dropouts) 564 165
Dropouts missing from our database 399 (no record at all)

The two counts of complete responses do not refer to exactly the same people:

  • What happened: the survey connected to Supabase through the transaction pooler (port 6543). In this mode, queries sent at the same time by different sessions can get mixed up. At 17:14 on October 30, one of these mix-ups left the database connection of an R process stuck. From then on, that process could no longer write to the database, while the survey kept working for the respondents:
    • Respondents already answering stopped being saved without any visible error, because surveydown catches failed writes and discards them without retrying. That is why 50 respondents finished the survey (and Netquest counts them as complete) but only part of their answers was stored. 7 more have no record at all.
    • New respondents assigned to a stuck process could not start the survey: the Connect Cloud log records 2,673 failed session starts. These are the 399 dropouts with no record in our database, many of whom tried several times.
    • Connect Cloud kept starting new R processes as more people arrived (17 between 16:52 and 20:34), and each one worked until it hit its own mix-up. The stuck processes were only stopped at 09:49 on October 31, and the second wave ran without problems.
  • Data: answers that were not written at the time cannot be recovered. The 50 partial responses are kept and flagged as interrupted by a technical failure, not as dropouts.
  • Solution, before the next wave:
    • Upgrade Supabase to the paid Pro plan, and adjust the instance and the pool size to the expected number of simultaneous connections.
    • Switch the survey to the session pooler (port 5432). Each R connection then keeps its own Postgres connection, so queries can no longer get mixed up.
    • Resume a session from cookies only when the id_user in the link matches the one stored in the browser. Otherwise a new session is started.
    • Monitor the Connect Cloud log during the first hour of each wave, and restart the app if errors appear. Ask Netquest to send invitations in smaller batches.
    • A second soft launch with a new target of 500 complete responses to evaluate the changes.

4.3 Quality Control

The indicators below are computed on the 469 complete responses stored in the database since October 29, 2026, as of october 31 (245 in version A and 224 in version B).

4.3.1 Monitor indicators

The survey tracks five quality indicators for complete responses:

Indicator Definition Result
Attention check Leaves attention_check blank, as the question asks 79.3% pass
Manipulation check Correctly identifies the conjoint task (manip_check = split a scholarship between two applicants) 80.4% pass
Speeders Duration below one third of the median (16.6 min, so below 5.5 min) 3.4%
Straightlining Same answer in all valid items of the Deservingness battery (at least 10 items) 1.7%
High NS/NR More than 25% of the scale items answered “No sabe” or “Prefiero no responder” 1.3%

4.3.2 Response patterns

Careless response patterns in the Likert items (straight lines, zigzags or any repeated sequence) are detected with the R package responsePatterns. It combines two methods: the auto-correlations of each person’s answers across items (rp.acors()) and the search for repeated sequences (rp.patterns()). Three analyses are run:

  • All 37 Likert items in the order they were presented, separately for version A and version B.
  • The 15-item Deservingness battery on its own.

A case is flagged when it is at or above the 95th percentile of either method in at least one analysis. 52 cases are flagged (11.1%): 38 in one analysis and 14 in two. Since the cut-off is a percentile, about 5% of cases are always above it in each analysis. The flag identifies cases to review, not cases to exclude automatically.

4.3.3 Combined assessment

Flagged response patterns are related to the other indicators. Among flagged cases, 42.3% fail the attention check and 9.6% are speeders, compared with 18.0% and 2.6% among the rest.

Each complete response is classified by the number of careless-responding indicators it meets: failing the attention check, speeding, a flagged response pattern, straightlining and high NS/NR. The manipulation check is not counted, because it measures understanding of the conjoint task rather than careless responding; it is reported separately.

Classification Criterion Cases % Fail manipulation check
Valid No indicator 336 71.6% 58
Possible problem One indicator 97 20.7% 24
Problem Two or more indicators 36 7.7% 10
Total 469 100% 92

  • Problem: the most frequent combinations are attention check + response pattern (14), attention check + speeder (9), attention check + response pattern + straightlining (4) and attention check + speeder + response pattern (3).
